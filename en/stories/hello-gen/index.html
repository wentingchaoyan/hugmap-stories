HUGMAP · MEET THE BUDDIES

Hello,
I’m Gen.

Where shall we go?
I’ll come with you.

Open the book ↓

01

Come and play!

Here comes the ball!
I love playing with you.

And when there’s something new to try,
I’m always eager to get going.

↓

02

Oops! I’ve gone
too far ahead.

I’m already a few steps down.
I look back.
You’re lifting a foot to take a step.

Hang on. I’m here.
We don’t have to hurry.

↓

03

Let’s see what’s tricky.

I watch as you lift one foot.
How do you keep your balance?

I check the steps and the handrail.
Your family and I look for ways
to help you feel steadier.

↓

04

Let’s try something else.

Here’s a place to rest your hand.
I turn to the side, and you turn too.

There you go!
Is that a little easier?

↓

05

Let’s take a break.

We’ve been busy!
I sit down beside you.

I was going to say, “One more go!”
But there’s no need to rush.
We can rest here together.

↓

06

This time,
we’ll go at your pace.

You point to where you want to go.
I’m right beside you.

If you need a hand,
or a rest along the way,
we’ll work it out together.

↓

A note for grown-ups

Making room for
little adventures.

Gen loves being on the move. Sometimes he gets so excited that he rushes ahead. But the best part of any adventure is sharing it with a child—and seeing them enjoy it, too.

For families, joining in can come with worries: “What if my child falls?” “Will they be able to play with the others?” Drawing on physiotherapy, Gen pays attention to balance, the support a child needs and signs of tiredness. He works with the family to explore what makes movement easier.

A helping hand or a rest along the way can be part of the adventure. Gen wants to help each child explore what interests them, at their own pace. “Look how far we’ve come!” That’s a moment worth sharing.

This story is not a guide to practising on stairs. The support a child needs should be explored with a professional who knows them.
References: APCP: Paediatric physiotherapy / NHS: Support with stairs
