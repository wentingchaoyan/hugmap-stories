Body Wonders 1 · Sensory Processing

Risu Sensei,
what is my body
looking for?

“More.” “That's enough.”
My body has its own way of sensing the world.

Read the story ↓

1

Sometimes my body
does puzzling things.

I can't yet explain
why, either.

Maybe it is looking for
a feeling that is “just right.”

2

Ahh. Ooh.

I press around my throat
and enjoy how my voice changes.

The vibration and the unusual sound
feel like an interesting game to me.

But pressing hard on my throat is dangerous.
First, gently help me stop.

3

Grip.

I want to pull
someone's hair hard.

The feeling of gripping. The feeling of pulling.
It hurts them, but I don't understand that yet.

Gently stop my hand
and offer something safe to grip instead.

4

I fill
my whole mouth with food.

With only a little, it can be hard
to feel exactly where
the food is in my mouth.

With a full mouth,
I can feel it strongly.

5

“Don't press your throat.”
“Let go of the hair.”
“Don't stuff your mouth.”

Mom is worried too.
I don't know how to stop.

I'm not trying to cause trouble.
My body is communicating something.

6

Risu Sensei
doesn't immediately decide
that sensory needs explain it.

“When? Where?
What happened before and after?
Let's look together.”」

7

A body's signals can have
many possible meanings.

Wanting more sensation?
Overwhelmed by too much?
Tired? Bored?
In pain? Finding something difficult?

Don't give one behavior
only one answer.
MoreToo muchPainHard

8

A full mouth raises
another important question.

Kaba Sensei looks at bite size,
chewing, tongue movement, and eating speed,
and whether I swallow without choking.

Not only sensation—
eating skills and safety too.

9

A chair where my feet touch the floor.
One bite at a time on the plate.

A short wait before the next bite.
A quiet place on days when sounds and smells are strong.

Instead of only stopping me,
make it safe and easier to sense.

10

Sometimes I repeatedly seek
sounds and vibration.

Grinding my teeth.
Tapping a sound-making toy again and again.
A sound that comes back the same way is predictable.

Stop what is unsafe
and look for safer sensory alternatives.

11

Risu Sensei
doesn't decide alone.

Kaba Sensei helps with eating.
Kuma Sensei helps with pain and health.
Mom's observations about what is unusual matter too.

When everyone's observations connect,
my signals come into view.

My behavior
wasn't meant to cause trouble.

“I need more sensation.”
“This is too much.”
“Something hurts.” “This is hard.”

They were different stories
told by my body.

After the story

Understanding a behavior and stopping it safely can happen together.

Even if pressing the throat, pulling another person's hair, or persistent tooth grinding may be sensory-seeking, stop the action safely first. Seek medical care for throat pain, hoarseness, or changes in breathing or swallowing, and dental advice for persistent grinding. Do not assume that overfilling the mouth is sensory alone; check chewing, tongue movement, bite size, posture, eating speed, and swallowing. Observe what happens before and after, and work with an occupational therapist, feeding or swallowing specialist, and healthcare professionals on safe alternatives.
